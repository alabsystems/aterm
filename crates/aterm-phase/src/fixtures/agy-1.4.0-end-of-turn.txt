> Write about 300 words on how ocean tides work. Do not use any tools.

  Ocean tides—the rhythmic rising and falling of sea levels across the globe—are driven primarily by the
  gravitational pull of the Moon and the Sun, combined with the rotation of the Earth.

  Although the Sun is vastly more massive than the Moon, the Moon is much closer to Earth. Because gravitational
  force weakens rapidly with distance, the Moon exerts more than twice the tidal force on our oceans as the Sun does.
  As the Moon orbits Earth, its gravity pulls on the planet's water, drawing it outward into a bulge on the side of
  Earth closest to the Moon.

  Simultaneously, a second bulge of water forms on the exact opposite side of the planet. This happens because the
  Earth and Moon actually orbit a shared center of mass. On the side of Earth facing away from the Moon, the outward
  inertial (centrifugal) force of this orbital motion exceeds the Moon's weaker gravitational pull, pushing the water
  away from the Moon.

  As Earth rotates on its axis once every 24 hours, coastal areas pass through both of these tidal bulges and the
  troughs between them. Consequently, most coastlines experience two high tides and two low tides every lunar day
  (which lasts about 24 hours and 50 minutes due to the Moon's own movement in its orbit).

  The Sun modifies this daily cycle. Twice a month, during the new and full moon phases, the Sun, Earth, and Moon
  align. Their combined gravitational pulls reinforce one another, creating spring tides—when high tides are
  exceptionally high and low tides are unusually low. Conversely, during the first and third quarter moon phases, the
  Sun and Moon sit at right angles relative to Earth. The Sun’s gravity partially cancels out the Moon’s pull,
  producing moderate neap tides with a smaller difference between high and low water. Finally, local coastline shapes
  and ocean floor depth further amplify or dampen these global rhythms.
